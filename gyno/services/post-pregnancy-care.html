Post Pregnancy Care — Healing, Recovery & Renewed Strength

Bringing a baby into the world is extraordinary — and so is the journey of recovery that follows. At Aarohi Women's Clinic, Dr. Ananya Sharma provides dedicated postnatal care to support every new mother through physical healing, emotional well-being, and the joyful, challenging transition to motherhood.

Why Post-Pregnancy Care is Essential

The postpartum period — the "fourth trimester" — is a time of significant physical and emotional transition for the new mother. While all attention naturally focuses on the baby, the mother's health deserves equal care and attention. Neglecting postnatal recovery can lead to long-term complications including pelvic floor disorders, postpartum depression, chronic anaemia, and difficulties with breastfeeding.

At Aarohi Women's Clinic, our postnatal programme ensures that every mother receives the monitoring, support, and guidance she needs to heal fully, recover her strength, and thrive in her new role.

What Our Postnatal Care Programme Covers

  • Post-Delivery Check-up (6 weeks): A comprehensive examination of wound healing (episiotomy or C-section scar), uterine involution, cervical assessment, blood pressure, and blood tests.
  • Breastfeeding Support: Guidance on correct latch, milk supply, nipple pain management, mastitis prevention, and pumping — supporting exclusive breastfeeding for at least 6 months.
  • Postpartum Depression Screening: Using validated tools (Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale) to identify mothers at risk of postpartum depression or anxiety — with counselling and referral where needed.
  • Pelvic Floor Rehabilitation: Guidance on Kegel exercises and pelvic floor recovery to address urinary leakage, pelvic organ prolapse risk, and sexual discomfort after delivery.
  • Anaemia & Nutrition: Blood count monitoring, iron and calcium supplementation, and dietary advice to restore energy and nutritional reserves depleted during pregnancy and delivery.
  • Contraception After Delivery: Discussion and prescription of suitable postnatal contraception — progestogen-only pill, IUD, or other methods compatible with breastfeeding.
  • Thyroid Monitoring: Postpartum thyroiditis can develop in the first year after delivery — we screen and manage this when indicated.
  • C-Section Recovery: Specialised wound care, scar management, and advice on safe activity resumption after caesarean delivery.

Signs to Watch for After Delivery

  • Excessive or foul-smelling vaginal discharge (lochia) beyond 6 weeks
  • Fever above 38°C in the first 2 weeks postpartum
  • Wound redness, swelling, discharge, or opening at the episiotomy or C-section site
  • Severe headache, blurred vision, or swelling of hands/face (signs of postpartum preeclampsia)
  • Persistent sadness, inability to bond with baby, or thoughts of self-harm (postpartum depression)
  • Calf pain, leg swelling, or breathlessness (possible deep vein thrombosis)
  • Difficulty urinating, pain on urination, or urinary incontinence
  • Breast pain, redness, fever, or hard lump in the breast (mastitis)

FAQs

When should I come for my first postnatal check-up?
+
Your first postnatal visit should be at 6 weeks after delivery. However, if you experience any concerning symptoms — fever, excessive bleeding, wound problems, or signs of postpartum depression — please come in or call us immediately, without waiting for the 6-week mark. Early problems need prompt attention.
How long does postpartum recovery take?
+
Physical recovery from a normal delivery typically takes 4–6 weeks. C-section recovery usually takes 6–8 weeks before returning to light activities, and up to 3 months for full physical recovery. Emotional recovery and hormonal stabilisation can take up to 12 months. Rest, good nutrition, and support from family and your medical team are essential during this time.
Is it normal to feel sad after having a baby?
+
Mild "baby blues" — feelings of tearfulness, irritability, and mood swings in the first 1–2 weeks — are very common and resolve on their own. However, if these feelings persist beyond 2 weeks, are severe, or include persistent sadness, inability to care for your baby, or thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, you may have postpartum depression, which requires professional support. Please reach out to us — postpartum depression is a medical condition, not a personal weakness.
