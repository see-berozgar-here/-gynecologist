📅 Menstrual Health

Period Problems — Restore Normal, Pain-Free Menstrual Health

Irregular periods, severe cramps, heavy bleeding, or missed cycles — these are not things you simply have to "live with." At Aarohi Women's Clinic, Dr. Ananya Sharma provides thorough diagnosis and effective treatment for all menstrual disorders.

Types of Period Problems We Treat

  • Dysmenorrhoea (Painful Periods): Primary dysmenorrhoea (no underlying cause) and secondary dysmenorrhoea caused by endometriosis, fibroids, or adenomyosis.
  • Menorrhagia (Heavy Bleeding): Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour, passing large clots, or periods lasting more than 7 days.
  • Oligomenorrhoea (Infrequent Periods): Cycles longer than 35 days, often linked to PCOS or thyroid disorders.
  • Amenorrhoea (Absent Periods): Primary (never started by age 15) or secondary (stopped for 3+ months) — requires thorough hormonal evaluation.
  • Metrorrhagia (Irregular Bleeding): Bleeding between periods — may indicate polyps, fibroids, or cervical conditions.
  • Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS): Severe mood changes, bloating, breast tenderness, and fatigue in the week before periods.
  • PMDD (Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder): A severe form of PMS with significant emotional and functional impact requiring medical management.

Common Causes of Menstrual Disorders

  • Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) — most common hormonal cause of irregular periods
  • Thyroid dysfunction — both hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism disrupt cycles
  • Endometriosis — uterine tissue growing outside the uterus causing pain and heavy bleeding
  • Uterine fibroids or polyps — benign growths causing heavy or irregular bleeding
  • Adenomyosis — uterine muscle affected by endometrial tissue, causing heavy, painful periods
  • Stress, extreme weight changes, or excessive exercise disrupting hormonal axis
  • Bleeding disorders or anaemia
  • Use of certain medications or contraceptives

Our Diagnostic Approach

  • Detailed menstrual and medical history taking
  • Hormonal blood panel — FSH, LH, estradiol, progesterone, thyroid, prolactin, testosterone, insulin
  • Pelvic ultrasound — uterus and ovaries assessment
  • Complete blood count to assess anaemia from heavy bleeding
  • Pap smear and cervical evaluation if irregular bleeding is present
  • Endometrial biopsy when indicated (in perimenopausal women or with abnormal bleeding)

FAQs

Are painful periods normal?
+
Mild cramping is normal for some women. However, severe pain that interferes with daily activities, requires strong painkillers, or is progressively worsening over time is NOT normal and deserves investigation. It may indicate endometriosis, adenomyosis, or fibroids — all of which are treatable conditions.
Can heavy periods cause anaemia?
+
Yes — heavy menstrual bleeding is one of the leading causes of iron-deficiency anaemia in women of reproductive age. Symptoms include fatigue, dizziness, breathlessness, and pale skin. Dr. Sharma will check your haemoglobin and ferritin levels and manage both the anaemia and the underlying cause of heavy bleeding.
How many days should a normal period last?
+
A normal menstrual period lasts 3 to 7 days, with a cycle length of 21 to 35 days. Blood loss of 30–80ml per period is considered normal. If your period consistently lasts more than 7 days, occurs more frequently than every 21 days, or involves very heavy bleeding, it is worth seeing a gynecologist for evaluation.
