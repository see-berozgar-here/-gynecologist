What is Antenatal / Pregnancy Care?

Antenatal care — also known as prenatal care — is the healthcare you receive during pregnancy. Regular antenatal check-ups are essential for monitoring your health and your baby's growth, detecting any complications early, and preparing you for a safe delivery and healthy postnatal recovery.

At Aarohi Women's Health & Maternity Clinic, we follow a structured, evidence-based pregnancy monitoring protocol that is fully customised to each patient's unique health history, lifestyle, and risk factors.

Common Pregnancy Concerns We Address

  • Morning sickness and severe nausea (hyperemesis gravidarum)
  • Gestational diabetes (high blood sugar during pregnancy)
  • Preeclampsia (pregnancy-induced hypertension)
  • Anemia and iron/folate deficiency during pregnancy
  • Thyroid disorders complicating pregnancy
  • Placenta previa or low-lying placenta
  • Preterm labour risk and cervical incompetence
  • Fetal growth restriction or abnormality detection
  • Multiple pregnancies (twins / triplets)
  • Previous history of miscarriage or preterm birth

Our Pregnancy Care Approach

We follow a trimester-wise structured care protocol:

  • First Trimester (Weeks 1–12): Pregnancy confirmation, blood work (HCG, thyroid, blood group), folic acid prescription, dating scan (7–8 weeks), NT scan at 11–13 weeks, counselling on diet, rest, and lifestyle.
  • Second Trimester (Weeks 13–27): Anomaly scan at 18–20 weeks, targeted blood tests, gestational diabetes screening, iron & calcium supplementation, fetal heart monitoring, and emotional well-being support.
  • Third Trimester (Weeks 28–40): Bi-weekly monitoring, growth scan, Doppler studies, assessment of baby's presentation, birth plan discussion, and preparation for hospital admission.

Benefits of Early and Consistent Pregnancy Care

  • Early detection of pregnancy complications before they become serious
  • Better birth outcomes and reduced risk of preterm delivery
  • Proper nutritional guidance for optimal fetal brain and organ development
  • Timely vaccinations (TT, flu) to protect mother and baby
  • Mental health support and anxiety management during pregnancy
  • Guidance on safe exercise, sleep positions, and travel during pregnancy
  • Detailed birth planning — normal delivery vs. planned C-section

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I visit a gynecologist after a positive pregnancy test?
+
You should book your first antenatal appointment as soon as you get a positive pregnancy test — ideally between 6 and 8 weeks of pregnancy. Early registration allows us to confirm the pregnancy, detect any complications, and start essential supplements like folic acid promptly.
How many ultrasound scans are needed during pregnancy?
+
A routine pregnancy typically requires 3–5 key scans: a dating scan (7–8 weeks), NT scan (11–13 weeks), anomaly scan (18–20 weeks), growth scan (28–30 weeks), and a final scan near term (34–36 weeks). Additional scans may be required if complications arise.
Is it safe to exercise during pregnancy?
+
Yes — light to moderate exercise is generally safe and beneficial during pregnancy. Walking, prenatal yoga, and swimming are excellent choices. However, always consult Dr. Sharma before starting any new exercise routine, especially if you have a high-risk pregnancy.
What vaccinations are required during pregnancy?
+
Two key vaccinations are recommended: the Tetanus Toxoid (TT) vaccine — given at the 16th and 20th week of a first pregnancy — and the seasonal flu vaccine, which protects both mother and baby. Dr. Sharma will guide you on the appropriate vaccination schedule.
Can I choose between normal delivery and C-section?
+
In most cases, the mode of delivery is guided by medical indications — the baby's position, placenta location, your health, and previous delivery history. Dr. Sharma will thoroughly discuss your options and create a birth plan that prioritises the safety of both mother and baby while respecting your preferences.
