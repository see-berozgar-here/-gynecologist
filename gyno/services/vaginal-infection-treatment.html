Vaginal Infection Treatment — Confidential, Compassionate Care

Vaginal infections are common, highly treatable, and nothing to be embarrassed about. At Aarohi Women's Clinic, Dr. Ananya Sharma provides completely private, stigma-free diagnosis and effective treatment to restore your comfort and health.

Types of Vaginal Infections We Treat

  • Bacterial Vaginosis (BV): The most common vaginal infection — caused by an overgrowth of anaerobic bacteria. Symptoms include a thin grey or white discharge with a characteristic fishy odour, especially after intercourse.
  • Vaginal Candidiasis (Yeast Infection / Thrush): Caused by Candida fungus overgrowth. Presents with thick, white, cottage-cheese-like discharge, intense itching, burning, and redness.
  • Trichomoniasis: A sexually transmitted infection (STI) caused by Trichomonas vaginalis. Symptoms include frothy yellow-green discharge with a strong odour, itching, and discomfort during urination.
  • Cervicitis: Inflammation of the cervix — often caused by chlamydia, gonorrhoea, or other pathogens. May present with abnormal discharge, intermenstrual bleeding, or pelvic pain.
  • Atrophic Vaginitis: Vaginal inflammation due to reduced estrogen levels — most common after menopause. Causes dryness, irritation, and painful intercourse.
  • Recurrent Vaginal Infections: Women who experience 4 or more yeast infections per year or recurring BV require deeper evaluation for underlying causes and long-term management.

Symptoms That Require Medical Evaluation

  • Unusual vaginal discharge — change in colour, texture, consistency, or volume
  • Vaginal or vulval itching, burning, or irritation
  • Unpleasant or unusual vaginal odour
  • Pain or discomfort during sexual intercourse (dyspareunia)
  • Burning sensation during urination
  • Vaginal bleeding between periods or after intercourse
  • Pelvic pain or lower abdominal discomfort
  • Vaginal or vulval swelling, redness, or skin lesions

Our Diagnosis & Treatment Approach

  • Clinical History: Full sexual health history, contraceptive use, menstrual history, and prior infections — taken confidentially and without judgement.
  • Vaginal Swab & Microscopy: Samples taken from the vagina and cervix for wet mount microscopy and culture to identify the causative organism.
  • STI Screening: Where clinically indicated — chlamydia, gonorrhoea, hepatitis B, and syphilis testing with full confidentiality.
  • pH Testing: Simple vaginal pH measurement to differentiate bacterial from fungal infections.
  • Targeted Treatment: Appropriate antibiotic, antifungal, or antiviral medication — oral, topical, or combined — based on confirmed diagnosis.
  • Partner Notification: Sensitive guidance on partner testing and treatment for STIs when relevant.

FAQs

Can a vaginal infection go away on its own?
+
Some mild yeast infections may resolve with over-the-counter antifungal treatment. However, bacterial vaginosis, STIs, and recurrent infections require accurate diagnosis and prescription treatment. Using the wrong treatment can worsen the infection or delay correct management. It's always better to get properly diagnosed before treating.
Are vaginal infections a sign of poor hygiene?
+
Absolutely not. Vaginal infections are common and can affect any woman, regardless of hygiene practices. In fact, excessive washing or use of scented products can disturb the natural vaginal pH and microbiome, making infections more likely. The vagina is self-cleaning — gentle external washing with water is all that is needed.
Should my partner also be tested?
+
For bacterial vaginosis, partner treatment is not routinely recommended but may help in recurrent cases. For STIs like chlamydia, gonorrhoea, and trichomoniasis, partner testing and simultaneous treatment is essential to prevent re-infection. Dr. Sharma will advise you sensitively based on your specific diagnosis.
