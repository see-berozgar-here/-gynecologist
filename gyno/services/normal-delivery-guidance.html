Normal Delivery Guidance — Empowering You for a Natural Birth

At Aarohi Women's Clinic, we believe every woman deserves to experience the joy and empowerment of a natural birth, with the right preparation, support, and expert medical guidance every step of the way.

What is Normal Delivery (Vaginal Birth)?

Normal delivery, also known as vaginal delivery, is the natural process by which a baby is born through the birth canal. It is the safest and most recommended mode of delivery for women with uncomplicated pregnancies. Recovery is faster, and both mother and baby benefit from the natural birth process.

At Aarohi Clinic, Dr. Ananya Sharma educates and prepares mothers from the second trimester onward, so that when labour begins, you are confident, informed, and supported — not afraid.

Conditions That Support Normal Delivery

  • Baby in head-down (cephalic) presentation by 36 weeks
  • Normal placenta position (not placenta previa)
  • No severe complications like preeclampsia or cord prolapse
  • Adequate amniotic fluid and healthy fetal heart rate
  • No prior classical C-section scar
  • Mother's pelvis of adequate size for vaginal birth
  • No active vaginal infections or herpes outbreak at the time of delivery

Our Preparation Programme for Normal Delivery

  • Birthing Education Sessions: Learn about the stages of labour, pain management options, and what to expect in the delivery room.
  • Pelvic Floor Exercises: Targeted exercises to strengthen muscles needed for pushing and postpartum recovery.
  • Breathing Techniques: Evidence-based breathing and relaxation methods (Lamaze) to manage contractions effectively.
  • Birth Position Guidance: Learn optimal positions to facilitate baby's descent during active labour.
  • Labour Support Plan: We help you identify your birth support partner and prepare them for the process.
  • Pain Management Options: Discussion of epidural options, breathing, hydrotherapy, and other comfort measures.

Benefits of Normal Delivery

  • Faster postpartum recovery — most women are mobile within hours
  • Lower risk of infection and surgical complications
  • Baby benefits from exposure to maternal microbiome, boosting immunity
  • Stronger breastfeeding establishment due to natural hormonal response
  • Shorter hospital stay — typically 24–48 hours vs. 3–5 days for C-section
  • Reduced risk of breathing problems for newborn
  • Better emotional bonding and oxytocin surge immediately after birth

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I opt for normal delivery after a previous C-section?
+
In selected cases, vaginal birth after caesarean (VBAC) is possible. Dr. Sharma will evaluate your previous C-section type, the reason for it, and your current pregnancy to determine if VBAC is safe for you. It requires careful monitoring and is only recommended in appropriate medical settings.
How painful is normal delivery and how is pain managed?
+
Labour pain is significant but manageable with proper preparation. Options include breathing and relaxation techniques, epidural analgesia, spinal blocks, and entonox (gas and air). Dr. Sharma will discuss your preferences and create a pain management plan that respects your choices while ensuring safety.
At what week does normal delivery usually happen?
+
A full-term pregnancy is 37–42 weeks. Most normal deliveries occur between 38 and 41 weeks. If labour doesn't begin naturally by 41–42 weeks, Dr. Sharma may recommend medical induction to prevent complications for mother and baby.
What is episiotomy and is it always needed?
+
An episiotomy is a small surgical incision made at the vaginal opening to facilitate delivery. It is not routinely performed — only when medically necessary, such as when the baby needs to be delivered quickly or when a natural tear would be more severe. Perineal massage during the third trimester can reduce the need for episiotomy.
