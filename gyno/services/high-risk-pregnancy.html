High-Risk Pregnancy Management — Expert Care When It Matters Most

A high-risk pregnancy requires heightened vigilance, specialist expertise, and compassionate support. Dr. Ananya Sharma at Aarohi Clinic has managed hundreds of complex pregnancies with exceptional outcomes for both mother and baby.

What is a High-Risk Pregnancy?

A pregnancy is classified as high-risk when certain maternal, fetal, or obstetric factors increase the likelihood of complications for the mother, baby, or both. Being classified as high-risk does not mean your pregnancy will result in complications — it simply means that closer, more specialised monitoring and care are necessary to achieve the best outcome.

At Aarohi Women's Clinic, we treat every high-risk pregnancy with a structured, individualised management protocol, combining frequent monitoring, advanced diagnostic support, and a carefully designed delivery plan.

Common Conditions That Make a Pregnancy High-Risk

  • Gestational diabetes (GDM) — high blood sugar developing during pregnancy
  • Preeclampsia and pregnancy-induced hypertension
  • Previous history of preterm birth or miscarriage
  • Multiple pregnancies — twins, triplets, or higher-order multiples
  • Placenta previa, low-lying placenta, or placental abruption
  • Severe anaemia or haemoglobin disorders (thalassemia, sickle cell)
  • Pre-existing medical conditions — hypothyroidism, heart disease, kidney disease, epilepsy
  • Fetal growth restriction (IUGR) — baby not growing adequately
  • Previous C-section with subsequent pregnancy
  • Pregnancy in women above 35 years (advanced maternal age)
  • Oligohydramnios or polyhydramnios (abnormal amniotic fluid levels)
  • Infections such as TORCH, COVID-19, or hepatitis B during pregnancy

Our High-Risk Pregnancy Management Approach

  • Increased Monitoring Frequency: More frequent antenatal visits — fortnightly or even weekly — to track maternal and fetal wellbeing closely.
  • Advanced Ultrasound Surveillance: Detailed growth scans, Doppler blood flow studies, and biophysical profiles to assess fetal health.
  • Specialised Blood Work: Regular panels for blood sugar, blood pressure markers, kidney function, thyroid levels, and clotting profiles.
  • Multi-Disciplinary Coordination: Collaboration with endocrinologists, cardiologists, or nephrologists when co-existing conditions require specialist input.
  • Hospitalisation When Needed: Planned or emergency admission for conditions requiring bed rest, IV medication, or continuous fetal monitoring.
  • Delivery Planning: Detailed birth plan — timing, mode of delivery, and emergency preparedness — tailored to your specific risk profile.

Why Early Referral to a Specialist Is Critical

  • Early management of gestational diabetes reduces the risk of macrosomia (overly large baby) and birth injuries
  • Blood pressure monitoring prevents dangerous progression to severe preeclampsia or HELLP syndrome
  • Regular fetal growth surveillance ensures timely intervention if growth falters
  • Early detection of placenta previa allows for safe elective delivery planning
  • Cervical cerclage for incompetent cervix can prevent preterm birth when diagnosed early

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a high-risk pregnancy always result in a C-section?
+
Not necessarily. Many women with high-risk pregnancies go on to deliver vaginally. The mode of delivery depends on the specific risk factors, how the pregnancy progresses, and the baby's position and health at the time of delivery. Dr. Sharma will assess each case individually and choose the safest delivery option.
I have been diagnosed with gestational diabetes. What should I do?
+
Gestational diabetes is manageable with dietary changes, regular blood sugar monitoring, and sometimes medication or insulin. Dr. Sharma will create a personalised management plan and monitor your baby's growth closely. Most women with well-controlled GDM have healthy babies and deliveries.
I am pregnant with twins — what special care do I need?
+
Twin pregnancies require more frequent ultrasounds (every 2–4 weeks after 20 weeks), closer monitoring of both babies' growth and health, early screening for twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (for identical twins), earlier delivery planning, and hospital delivery with neonatal support available.
