What is PCOS / PCOD?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) — also called Polycystic Ovarian Disease (PCOD) — is a hormonal disorder in which the ovaries produce excess androgens (male hormones), leading to irregular periods, multiple small cysts on the ovaries, and a range of metabolic and reproductive symptoms.

PCOS is the most common endocrine disorder in women of reproductive age, affecting 15–20% of Indian women. While it cannot be "cured," it is highly manageable with the right combination of lifestyle changes, medication, and long-term monitoring.

Common Symptoms of PCOS / PCOD

  • Irregular, infrequent, or completely absent menstrual periods
  • Heavy or prolonged bleeding when periods do occur
  • Unwanted facial or body hair (hirsutism) — upper lip, chin, chest, abdomen
  • Acne and oily skin that is difficult to control
  • Scalp hair thinning or androgenic alopecia (female pattern hair loss)
  • Unexplained weight gain — particularly around the abdomen
  • Dark, velvety skin patches in neck or armpits (acanthosis nigricans) — sign of insulin resistance
  • Difficulty conceiving due to irregular or absent ovulation
  • Mood changes, anxiety, or depression — linked to hormonal imbalance
  • Multiple small fluid-filled cysts visible on ovarian ultrasound

Our PCOS Management Approach

  • Comprehensive Hormonal Evaluation: LH, FSH, testosterone, DHEAS, AMH, insulin, fasting glucose, thyroid panel, and prolactin levels.
  • Pelvic Ultrasound: Assessment of ovarian morphology, antral follicle count, and uterine lining.
  • Lifestyle Medicine: A personalised diet and exercise plan is the most powerful long-term PCOS treatment — often resulting in natural period regulation and improved fertility.
  • Hormonal Medication: Combined oral contraceptive pills (COCPs) or progesterone-only medication to regulate periods and reduce androgen levels.
  • Insulin Sensitisers: Metformin to address insulin resistance, which drives many PCOS symptoms.
  • Fertility Treatment: For women trying to conceive, ovulation induction with Letrozole or Clomiphene under follicular monitoring.
  • Acne & Hair Management: Anti-androgen medication (Spironolactone) and referral for dermatological support if needed.

Long-Term Health Risks of Untreated PCOS

  • Type 2 diabetes — insulin resistance in PCOS increases diabetes risk significantly
  • Cardiovascular disease — elevated cholesterol, hypertension, and metabolic syndrome
  • Endometrial cancer — due to lack of regular menstruation and unopposed estrogen
  • Infertility — irregular or absent ovulation severely impairs natural conception
  • Depression and anxiety — chronic hormonal imbalance affects mental health
  • Sleep apnea — more common in women with PCOS and obesity

Frequently Asked Questions

Can PCOS be cured permanently?
+
PCOS cannot be permanently cured but it can be effectively managed. With consistent lifestyle changes — maintaining healthy weight, regular exercise, low-sugar diet — many women see a dramatic improvement in symptoms and some even achieve regular cycles without ongoing medication. It is a lifelong condition that requires ongoing monitoring.
Will I be able to get pregnant if I have PCOS?
+
Yes — most women with PCOS can conceive with appropriate treatment. Ovulation induction medication, lifestyle optimisation, and — if needed — IUI or IVF can help achieve pregnancy. Early diagnosis and management significantly improve fertility outcomes in women with PCOS.
How is PCOS diagnosed?
+
PCOS is diagnosed based on the Rotterdam Criteria — requiring at least 2 of 3 features: irregular/absent ovulation, clinical or biochemical signs of high androgens, and polycystic ovarian appearance on ultrasound. A blood hormonal panel and pelvic ultrasound are the standard investigations.
