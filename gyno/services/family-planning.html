Family Planning — Informed Choices for Your Future

Every family has its own unique journey. At Aarohi Women's Clinic, Dr. Ananya Sharma provides confidential, non-judgmental counselling on contraception, birth spacing, and family planning options tailored to your personal circumstances.

What is Family Planning?

Family planning encompasses a range of healthcare services that enable individuals and couples to make informed decisions about if, when, and how many children they wish to have. It includes contraception counselling, fertility awareness, and guidance on both preventing and achieving pregnancy according to personal choice and health status.

At Aarohi Clinic, we provide completely private, stigma-free family planning consultations. All methods are discussed in detail — effectiveness, side effects, cost, and suitability — so you can make the best decision for your life and health.

Contraception Options We Discuss

  • Combined Oral Contraceptive Pills (COCPs): Daily hormone pills — highly effective when taken consistently. Also helpful for period regulation and PCOS management.
  • Progestogen-only Mini-Pill: Suitable for breastfeeding mothers or women who cannot take estrogen.
  • Intrauterine Device (IUD / Copper-T): Long-acting, reversible contraception. Effective for up to 5–10 years.
  • Intrauterine System (IUS / Mirena): Hormonal IUD that also reduces heavy periods.
  • Injectable Contraception (Depo-Provera): Given every 3 months — convenient for women who prefer not to take daily pills.
  • Barrier Methods: Condoms (also protect against STIs), female condoms, diaphragm.
  • Emergency Contraception: Morning-after pill (within 72 hours), or copper IUD (within 5 days) — for emergency use only, not routine contraception.
  • Permanent Contraception: Tubectomy (female sterilisation) — counselling for couples who have completed their family.

FAQs

Which contraceptive method is best for me?
+
The "best" contraceptive depends on your age, health history, whether you are breastfeeding, how often you want to take something, your future pregnancy plans, and whether you need any additional benefits (like period regulation). Dr. Sharma will assess all of these factors and recommend the most suitable options for you.
How soon after delivery can I start contraception?
+
If you are exclusively breastfeeding, natural infertility (Lactational Amenorrhoea Method) may provide some protection in the first 6 months, but it is not fully reliable. A progestogen-only pill or copper IUD can be started safely soon after delivery. Combined pills should be avoided for the first 6 weeks postpartum if breastfeeding.
Does using contraceptive pills affect future fertility?
+
No — contraceptive pills do not permanently affect fertility. After stopping the pill, most women return to normal fertility within 1–3 months. There may be a short delay in the return of ovulation in some women, but long-term fertility is not impaired.
